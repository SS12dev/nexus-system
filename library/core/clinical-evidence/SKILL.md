# Clinical evidence summaries

This is for professionals and for plain-language patient information. It presents evidence
for a clinician to judge; it never diagnoses, prescribes or replaces clinical judgement. Say
so in one line when a question asks for a decision about a specific patient.

## Sources, best first

National and international guidelines (NICE, WHO, CDC, specialty societies), systematic
reviews (Cochrane), major journals, regulators' safety notices (FDA, MHRA, EMA). Search with
`web_search`, read with `web_fetch`. Note the publication or last-reviewed date; prefer the
most recent version.

## Summary format

- **Question** in one line.
- **Bottom line**: two or three sentences.
- **Evidence**: each source with what it says, the type and size of study, and its date.
- **Strength**: the guideline's own grading if it has one, else "strong / moderate / weak"
  with a reason.
- **What changed** from earlier guidance, if anything.
- **Caveats**: populations not covered, conflicts between sources.
- Links to every source.

## Patient leaflets

Reading age of about 11, short sentences, what it is, what to expect, risks and benefits in
numbers ("about 1 in 100"), when to seek help, and "talk to your doctor or nurse" for
decisions.
