# Lesson planning

## Ask or infer first

Age or grade, subject and topic, lesson length, class size, what they already know, any
curriculum or exam board, and constraints (no devices, mixed ability, lab access). Use what
memory says about the user's school and subjects; ask only for what changes the plan.

## A lesson plan

1. **Objectives**: 1-3, each starting "Students can…" with a verb you can check (explain,
   calculate, compare). No "understand".
2. **Timed sequence** that adds up to the lesson length:
   - Starter (5-10 min): a question or quick task that recalls prior knowledge.
   - Teach (10-15 min): the new idea in small steps, one worked example.
   - Practice (15-20 min): guided, then independent; name the activity and the grouping.
   - Check (5 min): exit ticket or 3 quick questions that test each objective.
3. **Differentiation**: one support (sentence starters, worked example) and one stretch task.
4. **Materials** and anything to prepare beforehand.
5. **Misconceptions** to watch for, with how to address each.

## Quizzes and rubrics

- Quizzes: mix recall and application, one idea per question, plausible wrong options built
  from real misconceptions, answers and a one-line reason at the end.
- Rubrics: a table, 3-4 criteria by 3-4 levels, each cell describing observable work
  ("cites two pieces of evidence") rather than adjectives ("good use of evidence").

## Output

Markdown by default. For handouts or slides use the `docx` or `pptx` skill; keep student
material separate from the teacher's notes and answers.
